The Younger Dryas

The Younger Dryas card and the Magicians of the Gods card both open this page. The ice line is the cold snap on the record. The gold line is Graham Hancock's books, the modern reading that sets the flood stories, a comet, and this cold on the same mark.

About 12,900 to 11,700 years ago

The cold snap

The cold snap is the one read from ice cores and sediment layers. The Younger Dryas runs about 12,900 to 11,700 years ago, roughly 10,900 to 9,700 BCE, also written about 10,896 to 9,703 BCE.

It is a short, sharp return to cold at the very end of the glacial period, after the world had already begun to warm. The Last Glacial Period is the longer cold, about 115,000 to 11,700 years ago. When this snap ends, the warm Holocene begins, from about 11,700 years ago.

Greenland Ice Core Chronology 2005

The ice cores

Rasmussen and others, in the Journal of Geophysical Research (2006), build a common time scale for the NGRIP and GRIP ice cores and tie it to the DYE-3 core. They name it the Greenland Ice Core Chronology 2005. It covers 7.9 to 14.8 thousand years before present, and includes the Younger Dryas.

Their table dates the onset of the Younger Dryas to 12,896 years before A.D. 2000, with a maximum counting error of 138 years. It dates the Younger Dryas–Preboreal transition to 11,703 years before A.D. 2000, with a maximum counting error of 99 years. The paper reports those ages relative to A.D. 2000. From that zero, 12,896 years is 10,896 BCE, and 11,703 years is 9,703 BCE.

Rasmussen and others, 2006, Journal of Geophysical Research

11,703 years before A.D. 2000

The Holocene boundary

The same paper dates the Younger Dryas–Preboreal transition to 11,703 years before A.D. 2000. That transition is the end of this snap. On the timeline, the warm Holocene begins from about 11,700 years ago.

Rasmussen and others, 2006, Journal of Geophysical Research

9 October 2007

Firestone and others

R. B. Firestone, A. West, J. P. Kennett, L. Becker, T. E. Bunch, Z. S. Revay, P. H. Schultz, T. Belgya, D. J. Kennett, J. M. Erlandson, O. J. Dickenson, A. C. Goodyear, R. S. Harris, G. A. Howard, J. B. Kloosterman, P. Lechler, P. A. Mayewski, J. Montgomery, R. Poreda, T. Darrah, S. S. Que Hee, A. R. Smith, A. Stich, W. Topping, J. H. Wittke, and W. S. Wolbach. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 9 October 2007, volume 104, number 41, pages 16016–16021.

They write that they provide evidence for an extraterrestrial impact at about 12.9 thousand years ago, which they hypothesize contributed to Younger Dryas cooling. They propose that one or more large, low-density extraterrestrial objects exploded over northern North America, partially destabilizing the Laurentide Ice Sheet and triggering that cooling.

Firestone and others, PNAS 2007

2023

The debate

Vance T. Holliday and others, Earth-Science Reviews, volume 247 (2023), article 104502. Their abstract says a series of publications purport to provide evidence that Earth was subjected to an extraterrestrial event or events at about 12.9 thousand years ago, and that those speculations are known as the Younger Dryas Impact Hypothesis.

The same abstract says no craters have been identified that date to the onset of the Younger Dryas, and that their review finds the hypothesis should be rejected.

Holliday and others, Earth-Science Reviews, 2023 Open copy

Fingerprints of the Gods

The author's page titles it Fingerprints of the Gods, Evidence of Earth's Lost Civilization (1995). That page asks whether a global culture was destroyed at the end of the ice age, with remnants passing knowledge on. His books page argues for a maritime civilisation earlier than 12,000 years ago, wiped out by the sea-level rises that came with the melting ice sheets.

Penguin Random House lists a paperback of the same title, with the date 2 April 1996.

Underworld

The author's page titles it Underworld: Flooded Kingdoms of the Ice Age (2002). It describes a flood that swept the Earth at the end of the last Ice Age, and a search for underwater ruins of a lost civilization.

2015

Magicians of the Gods

The author's page calls Magicians of the Gods the sequel to Fingerprints of the Gods, and gives 10 September 2015 in the UK and 10 November 2015 in the US. The full title on that page is Magicians of the Gods: The Forgotten Wisdom of Earth's Lost Civilisation.

The page sets a giant comet, broken into fragments, near the end of the last Ice Age, 12,800 years ago. Some fragments strike the North American ice cap, and further fragments strike the northern European ice cap. It sets a second series of impacts at 11,600 years ago. The civilization, on that page, is destroyed between 12,800 and 11,600 years ago.

grahamhancock.com, Magicians of the Gods

His reading

Flood myths, a comet, and Plato

His books page reads more than 600 flood myths as memories of the end of the last Ice Age, earlier than 12,000 years ago, when a maritime civilisation was wiped out by rising seas.

The Magicians of the Gods page sets the comet fragments at 12,800 years ago, and the heat of those impacts as the cause of a global deluge remembered in myths. It sets a second series of impacts at 11,600 years ago, and calls that the date Plato gives for the destruction and submergence of Atlantis. Survivors, on that page, settle at Gobekli Tepe.

grahamhancock.com, Books grahamhancock.com, Magicians of the Gods

Their page

The Comet Research Group

The Comet Research Group's own page says that about 12,800 years ago a giant broken-up comet caused airbursts or craters across the Northern Hemisphere, deposited melted material in a Younger Dryas boundary layer, and melted parts of the ice sheets over Canada and Europe. The page says that event triggered the Younger Dryas cooling.

Comet Research Group, The Younger Dryas Impact

2017

Sweatman and Tsikritsis

Martin B. Sweatman and Dimitrios Tsikritsis, Mediterranean Archaeology and Archaeometry, volume 17, number 1 (2017). They read the Vulture Stone at Göbekli Tepe as a date stamp for 10950 BC, plus or minus 250 years, close to a Younger Dryas event they estimate at 10890 BC. They also read an interest there in the Taurid meteor stream, which they say is proposed as responsible for the Younger Dryas event.

Sweatman and Tsikritsis, 2017

16 November 2016

Joe Rogan Experience #872

On Joe Rogan Experience #872, with Randall Carlson, November 16, 2016, an automated transcript has him describe the Taurid stream as debris of a giant comet that entered the inner solar system about 20,000 years ago, broke into fragments, and still crosses Earth's path. Earth passes through that stream twice a year. He says pieces hit the North American ice cap 12,800 years ago, and again about 11,600 years ago. He ties the Tunguska explosion of June 30, 1908, to the June crossing. No official transcript. That line about the comet staying visible is Martin Sweatman's.

He tells the same comet story on #725, #1897, and #2051. On #1897 the automated transcript dates the breakup to 12,800 years ago, which disagrees with the 20,000-year entry on #872.
