Ancient timeline

Devas and Asuras

This page expands the timeline card. The gold line in the margin marks the myth side. The card follows the Deva–Asura wars in Vedic and Purāṇic tradition. The Kurukshetra war of the Mahābhārata, with the Bhagavad Gītā spoken on its eve, is a war of kings and armies with divine counsel; the sources for this page are the Deva–Asura conflicts instead.

Overview

Gods and anti-gods at war

In the Ṛgveda, Indra slays the dragon Vṛtra and releases the waters. In the Purāṇas, the Devas and Asuras (Daityas, Dānavas) fight repeatedly; after the Devas are worsted, Viṣṇu has them churn the Milk Ocean with the Asuras for the nectar of immortality (amṛta). The Devas drink; the Asuras are denied. That cycle is the clearest sustained gods-war for this card.

Ṛgveda 1.32 (Griffith) Viṣṇu Purāṇa, churning chapter Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.7

In the story Beat 1

Indra and Vṛtra

The Thunder-wielder slays the Dragon and opens the waters.

I WILL declare the manly deeds of Indra, the first that he achieved, the Thunder-wielder. He slew the Dragon, then disclosed the waters, and cleft the channels of the mountain torrents.

Ṛgveda 1.32.1, Ralph T. H. Griffith. sacred-texts.com.

In the story Beat 2

The Asuras make war

In McComas Taylor’s Viṣṇu Purāṇa translation, after Durvāsas’s curse drains the three worlds of prosperity, the Daityas and Dānavas make war against the deities. The gods, worsted, seek Brahmā, then Viṣṇu.

Viṣṇu Purāṇa ch. 9 (Taylor)

In the story Beat 3

Churning the Milk Ocean

Viṣṇu tells the gods to cast herbs into the Milky Ocean, take Mount Mandara as the churning-stick and the nāga Vāsuki as the cord, and churn with the Daityas for the nectar of immortality—while ensuring the enemies receive none.

Viṣṇu Purāṇa ch. 9

In the story Beat 4

Nectar, deceit, and the gods restored

When the Daityas seize the nectar pitcher, Viṣṇu appears in female form, takes it back for the gods. Indra and the hosts drink; the Daityas assail them; the deities, filled with joy, regain their realm.

Viṣṇu Purāṇa ch. 9

Composition or tablet date

When the text is dated

The Ṛgveda hymns are the oldest layer of this tradition; Griffith’s English is a public-domain Victorian translation. The Purāṇic churning episode is later narrative literature; the Taylor Viṣṇu Purāṇa and Bhāgavata Purāṇa pages linked above carry the English used for the summary.

Griffith Ṛgveda 1.32 Taylor Viṣṇu Purāṇa

Indra–Vṛtra quotation is Ralph T. H. Griffith’s Ṛgveda at sacred-texts.com. Samudra Manthan beats follow McComas Taylor’s Viṣṇu Purāṇa translation on wisdomlib.org.
