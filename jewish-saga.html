The family

Abraham through Rome

Traditional maximalist reading. Isaac: 2066 to 1886 BCE. Jacob: 2006 to 1859 BCE. Jacob enters Egypt 1876 BCE at age 130, and the twelve sons are already that household. A looser window often cited is about 2100 to 1500 BCE. Abraham on this page stays about 2000 to 1800 BCE. Critical scholars since Thomas L. Thompson and John Van Seters generally do not date Isaac or Jacob as historical figures.

Abraham

Traditional chronologyAbout 2000 to 1800 BCE

Abraham leaves Ur

A man leaves the city of Ur. The promise is a land, a people, and a blessing that will run out to the nations. The line that reaches Christ is this one.

Traditional chronologyAfter the call

The covenant

The promise is cut and the household is marked off from the nations that scattered after Babel. From here the story is no longer about the whole world at once. It follows one family.

Traditional chronologyThe next generation

Isaac

The son of the promise, born against the odds. The covenant passes to him, not to every child of Abraham.

Traditional maximalist reading: 2066 to 1886 BCE.

Traditional chronologyThe next generation

Jacob and the twelve tribes

Jacob, later called Israel, fathers the twelve. The people who will go down into Egypt, and come back out, are all in that family.

Traditional maximalist reading: 2006 to 1859 BCE.

Enslaved

Traditional chronologyThe centuries in Egypt

Slavery in Egypt

The family becomes a nation inside Egypt, and then a nation enslaved. This is the Egypt of the oppression.

Moses leads them out

Traditional chronologyAbout 1446 BCE, the early date

Moses and the Exodus

Moses leads them out. The sea closes on the pursuit. They leave as the people of the covenant, with the wilderness in front of them. The later date, in the 1200s BCE, is the main alternative. This page uses the early one.

Traditional chronologyThe same season

The Law at Sinai

The mountain smokes. The law is given: who they are, and how a people taken out of Egypt is to live.

The long wandering

Traditional chronologyForty years

The wilderness

The generation that would not enter the land dies on the way. Their children grow up facing the Jordan.

They found Israel

Traditional chronologyAbout 1400 BCE

Joshua and Canaan

They cross the river. Jericho falls. The land named to Abraham is taken and divided among the tribes.

They fight over Israel for a long time

Traditional chronologyThe long middle

The judges

A rough, uneven age. Deliverers rise when the people are overrun, and the land keeps slipping back. There is still no king.

Traditional chronologyAbout 1050 BCE

Saul

The first king. He is anointed, he wins, and he fails. The kingdom is already looking past him.

Traditional chronologyAbout 1010 BCE

David

The shepherd king takes Jerusalem and holds the kingdom. The promise narrows again: a son of David, and his throne.

Traditional chronologyAbout 970 BCE, Temple about 960

Solomon and the Temple

The kingdom is at its widest. A house is built in Jerusalem for the name that had lived in a tent since Sinai.

Traditional chronologyAbout 930 BCE

The kingdom splits

After Solomon the kingdom tears. Israel in the north, Judah in the south. Two thrones, and one story coming apart.

Traditional chronology722 BCE

Assyria takes the north

Assyria destroys the northern kingdom and deports Israel. Judah is what remains.

Traditional chronology586 BCE

The Babylonian exile

Babylon burns the Temple Solomon built and carries Judah away. Jerusalem is empty, and the line of David looks finished.

Traditional chronology538 BCE

The return under Cyrus

Cyrus of Persia lets the exiles go home. They come back to a ruined city, not to the kingdom they lost.

Traditional chronologyAbout 516 BCE

The Second Temple

A new house rises on the old ground, poorer than Solomon's. It is the Temple still standing, rebuilt again in a later age, when Rome arrives.

Traditional chronology167 BCE

The Maccabean revolt

When the Temple is desecrated, a revolt takes it back. For a short while Judah rules itself. It does not last.

Traditional chronology63 BCE

Rome

Pompey takes Jerusalem. The land of the promise is under Rome, and the throne of David is not on it.
